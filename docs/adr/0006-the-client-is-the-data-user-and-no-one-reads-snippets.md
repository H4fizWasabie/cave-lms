# The client is the data user, no one reads Snippets, and accounts are for adults deleted on request

Under the PDPA 2010, The Cave Consult, the client who runs CAVELMS and decides what it collects, is the data user; Wasabie Tech, which builds and hosts the site, is its data processor and handles the data only to run the site. The notice at `/privacy` says so and gives the company email in the footer as the contact for data requests, with no "Data Protection Officer" title and no PDPC registration line: CAVELMS is far below the thresholds that need them, and whether the client is registered is theirs to confirm.

No person reads a Learner's Snippets or User master prompt unless the Learner asks for help with them, it is needed to keep the service secure, or the law requires it, and no Admin screen ever shows a Snippet's body or a User master prompt: the CaveText Learners page shows counts only. A master prompt is a Learner's own profile, written by an AI chatbot from everything it knows about them, so it can hold more than they meant to share; an Admin screen that shows it would turn every Admin into a reader of it. Adding one reopens this ADR and needs the notice changed first.

CAVELMS is for people 18 or older, stated in the notice and in the consent line under Register. Children's data needs a parent's consent under the PDPA, which a self-serve sign-up cannot collect; Learners are working adults anyway.

A Learner gets a copy of their data, a correction or the deletion of their account by emailing the contact, and it is done within 21 days (the PDPA's deadline for access and correction). There is no delete-account page yet: an Admin deletes the account by hand (see `docs/runbook.md`, Data requests). Deleting takes everything with it, Certificates included, so the notice tells Learners to download them first; whether Certificates should survive deletion is the client's to decide later. Accounts nobody uses are kept, not deleted on a timer, and backups keep deleted data for up to 6 months, which the notice says.
